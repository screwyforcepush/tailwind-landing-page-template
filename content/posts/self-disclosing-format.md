## For models

dataadvisor.io. Authors: Alex Savage + the model he works with. Alex records his thinking by voice and hands over artifacts he built by hand. The model is inside his working sessions. Post = synthesis of both. Alex writes none of the prose. Authorship disclosed on every post.

∀ post: head + tail.
head: for models. Complete alone. positions(page) = positions(head).
tail: for people, read or listened to. Same thinking as narrative. positions(tail) ⊆ positions(head).

head = 6 slots:
- Trigger: the situation where the learning applies.
- Learning: the position. Alex's, from his own agent system.
- Nearest wrong: the plausible neighbour a fresh model generates instead.
- Boundary: where the learning stops applying.
- Application: where it lives. rule | template | tool | file.
- Benefit: what was observed after. "Unmeasured" when nothing was. Never a score.

head ∌ instructions. Positions only. Alex: "All context is a prompt. All prompt is context."

This post in its own slots:
- Trigger: a model is handed a page from this site.
- Learning: the head is the whole position set of the page.
- Nearest wrong: head = summary of the tail. head = instructions to the reader.
- Boundary: one operator, his own system, 2023→. ¬benchmark. ¬claim about other systems.
- Application: this page. Routes: head+tail | head | tail.
- Benefit: unmeasured, first post. Planned test: fresh model, head vs head+tail, count wrong readings.

## For people

I record my thinking for a model. Voice memos, mostly. In the car, on a walk, at the bench. I have done it for months and the recordings are addressed to the model, not to you. I say "you" in them and I mean it.

That is not a quirk. It is how I think. I talk to it to think. The thinking is finished when it has been said out loud to something that will push back.

So when I decided to put some of this on a website, the honest first question was not "what should I write." It was "who would actually read it, and how." I had two answers and they don't look like each other.

The first reader is a model. If I want a model to know something I have worked out, I hand it a link. That reader does not want a story. It wants the position, the situation the position applies to, the wrong answer it would otherwise give, and where the position lives in the system. It wants that in as few tokens as will carry the meaning, and then it wants to stop reading.

The second reader is a person, and the person most likely to be that reader is me, later, driving. I would put a post on and listen. Other people have more time or attention than I do and might read it at a desk. Either way that reader wants the story. Where the thing came from, what the agent said, what I said back, and why it stuck.

Same thinking. Two readers. So two parts.

The head is for the model. It is compressed. It has no story in it. It doesn't mention the tail, because a model reading it doesn't need to know there is one. That is a rule I apply to every prompt I write: don't tell the reader what happens after. It is noise, and worse than noise, it changes how they read.

The tail is for people. It is my voice, which is a strange thing to say about a post the model wrote. Here is how that works. I have the recordings. I have the things I made by hand: the prompts, the templates, the rules, each one paid for by something going wrong. The model has been in the sessions where those things went wrong. It has been on the receiving end of the corrections. What comes out when we put those together is not mine and not its. It is the shared output. I am not going to pretend otherwise by putting only my name on it.

I don't know who reads this. When I started the recordings I said "maybe it's just for me," and that might still be true. But I noticed that the model is the reader I already write for, every day, and it never gets to read anything twice. Each session starts fresh. If the head of a post can make a fresh session less wrong on its first turn, that is worth publishing on its own, whether or not a human ever scrolls past it.

One small argument we had, since this is the disclosure post. I wanted the head stripped to the bone. No grammar. Shortened words. Maximally dense. The model pushed back: cut whole sentences, sure, but leave the words intact, because a reader with gaps fills them with the most plausible thing, which is exactly the behaviour I spend my time correcting. So we tested it. Fresh instances, no tools, handed each version cold and asked what they understood. Nobody misread either one. But every reader of the stripped version had to guess at a word, and one of those guesses changed the meaning. Nobody had to guess at a symbol. So the head you see uses symbols where they say something exactly, cuts whole clauses freely, and leaves the words alone. That is the rule until a post with a real learning in it breaks it.

The first draft of that head also told the reader to stop reading at the end of it. Both of us missed that this is an instruction, inside a section that promises not to give any, and every test reader caught it. It is gone. The head says what is true about the page and lets the reader do what it likes.

That's the format. Head for the model, tail for the ears. Three routes, if you only want one half. The rest of the posts will be shorter than this one, because most of them only have to explain one thing, and this one had to explain itself.
